Module 1 · Strategy

Who relies on Ascend IQ, and what a wrong answer costs

Ascend IQ lets clients ask the Ascend Analytics platform plain-language questions and get answers in seconds instead of digging through it.

Who it's for

VP-level strategists and product leaders at Fortune 500 companies who pay $50,000+ a year for verified market intelligence.

What they ask

"Compare the pricing models of Stripe and Adyen." Complex, multi-part questions, sometimes with typos, that feed strategic decisions.

What's at stake

Engagement is dropping and Q4 retention is at risk. The CPO wants it live for the top 50 enterprise accounts. One poor answer to the wrong client can do lasting damage.

For all executive clients, Ascend IQ promises a verifiable, clear and brief response in about 5 seconds, without manual data mining, so that Ascend Analytics can meet its Q4 retention milestone.

Module 1 · Strategy

Three trust metrics, and what we give up for them

Each metric is tied to the promise. When they conflict, being correct wins over being fast.

Hallucination rate

% of answers that are confidently false or fabricated.

Clients pay for verified data. One fabricated statistic can put the whole contract at risk.

Robustness

Coherent, correct answers on messy, multi-part or out-of-scope questions.

VPs ask complex questions with typos and still expect a clear, verifiable answer.

Latency

P95 response time ≤ 5 seconds.

Ascend IQ has to stay faster than searching the platform by hand.

Trade-off 1 · Hallucination over Latency

A slower but accurate answer builds trust. A fast wrong one can cost a $100,000 contract.

Trade-off 2 · Robustness over Latency

When the question is messy, the answer must still be clear and unambiguous, even if it takes longer.

Module 5 · Scale

Coverage at portfolio scale: three honest gaps

Applied to a second product line, AI Report Summaries (executive summaries sent straight to clients), only one risk is fully covered today.

✅Hallucination

Judge vs source report, every release

⚠️Latency

Logged, but no target

❌Fairness

Not tested across clients or regions

❌UX trust

Tone not checked

❌Robustness

Messy reports not tested

Accepted gap · Fairness

We target the European market and write in English only, so a fix would cost more than the risk today.

Kill criterion: non-English-speaking clients pass 25% of our clients, or clients ask for other languages.

Cannot accept · Robustness

A broken summary of a messy report, sent to a paying client, puts the renewal at risk.

Plan: a messy-report test set every release must pass, plus a weekly LLM judge on live summaries. QA team, 30 Nov 2026.

Reflection

What defining "good enough" forced me to confront

$10

The gap between $49 and $59 is enough to break a promise of verified data. "Good enough" has to be set per failure, not as one overall score.

1 of 8

Each pricing check caught one hallucination. A check covers the failure it was written for, and nothing else.

3 of 20

The judge calls I overrode. The judge needs checking too.

The hardest part was accepting that "good enough" sometimes means telling the CPO "not yet" when the deadline is close and the numbers say no.
